# AGENTS.md — bringing an LLM up to speed on Proximate

Orientation for an AI assistant (or a new human) picking this project up cold. Read this
before proposing changes. `CLAUDE.md` holds the short command reference; this file explains
the *why*, and the traps.

---

## 1. What this is

A **one-knob proximity effect compensator** for speech and vocal microphones, built with
JUCE in C++, shipping as VST3, AU and Standalone from one target. It measures the balance
between the energy under 250 Hz and the energy over it, holds that balance at or under a
ceiling the knob sets, and takes the excess off with a first-order low shelf whose shape is
the inverse of a directional microphone's proximity rise.

Public repo, MIT, ships a user-facing AI-assisted disclaimer in the README. Version 0.1.0.
Never used on a real microphone.

## 2. The rules that matter most

**It never boosts.** The shelf is cut-only, the high band passes at unity, and amount 0 is
bit-for-bit transparent. `tests/test_compensator.cpp` checks the shelf at its maximum depth
at eight frequencies and the transparency sample for sample. A makeup gain, a "restore the
low end when they step back" option, a smoothing that overshoots — none of these may exist.

**It has zero latency.** No look-ahead, no internal buffering, no oversampling.
`prepareToPlay` reports 0 and pluginval checks it. The impulse test asserts the impulse
comes out on its own sample.

**It reacts to balance, not level.** The detector is a ratio. If a change makes the same
signal at −32 dBFS get a different cut from the one at −12 dBFS, it has broken the product.
There is a test.

**One knob.** The tuning in `dsp::Tuning` is fixed on purpose. A threshold, a ratio, an
attack and a release would make this a dynamic EQ, and the world has those. Change a tuning
value if the measurements say so, then change the guide's table and the tests' numbers with
it; do not expose it.

**`DSP/` depends on nothing but the standard library, and that is load-bearing.** It is why
the suite runs without a host, a device or a microphone, and why `proxstat` can print the
numbers the README quotes. If something in there starts needing `juce_core`, it belongs
somewhere else.

## 4. How it works, and the traps

### 4.1 The detector is a balance, not a level

Two 2nd-order Butterworth filters split the mono sum at 250 Hz. They are
power-complementary (|L|² + |H|² = 1), so the split loses nothing. Each band is squared
and smoothed over 200 ms; the balance is the ratio in dB. The 200 ms is the important
number: with 20 ms the balance of speech at a fixed distance spreads about 10 dB between
its 10th and 90th percentile — it follows vowels — while with 200 ms it spreads about 3 dB
and the 4–7 dB shift from a talker at 4 cm survives. This was measured on four `say` voices
through a cardioid model before any code was written; `Tuning::integrationMs` documents it.

A separate 10 ms envelope on the high band gates the detector. When it is under −50 dBFS
the *target* freezes (not the cut, which finishes settling onto it). The gate is fast on
purpose: gating on the 200 ms detectors let the cut drift for a second and a half while
they decayed through the threshold — visible in the first version of the hold test.

### 4.2 The shelf's zero is fixed and its pole moves

`H(s) = (s + ω_z) / (s + ω_z / g)`: zero at 120 Hz, DC gain `g`, pole `g` times higher.
A deeper cut reaches further up the spectrum, which is the inverse of a proximity rise
whose corner climbs as the talker gets closer. At 12 dB the pole is at 480 Hz and the
shelf reads −10/−6/−2.6/−0.8 dB at 100/250/500/1000 Hz — a handheld dynamic's published
proximity curve at a few centimetres, inverted, to within a dB.

The first version centred the shelf on the split with the pole and zero moving apart
symmetrically. It was wrong in both directions: shallow near the split, where a voice's
low-band energy actually sits, and *narrower* as it deepened. The balance of a female
voice barely moved for a 3 dB cut. Do not go back to it.

Implementation is a TPT one-pole per channel, `y = hp + g·lp`, with the coefficient set
every sample from the smoothed cut. The topology's state does not depend on the
coefficient, so the pole can move every sample without a transient. Under 1e-4 dB of cut
the sample is passed through untouched rather than as `(x − lp) + lp`, which is not always
`x` to the last bit — that is what makes amount 0 bit-exact.

### 4.3 The ratio is 2, and the ceiling is what the knob sets

The gain computer is `cut = ratio × softknee(balance − ceiling)`, capped at 18 dB, with
`ratio = 2`. Not 1: the balance the detector measures moves by only a quarter to a third
of the shelf's nominal depth (the low band's energy is near the split, where the shelf is
shallow, and the high band loses a little too), and physically a 6 dB tip of the balance
comes from a boost of about 12 dB at 100 Hz. Ratio 3 was considered and rejected: the 3 dB
of ordinary phonetic wobble would become 9 dB of cut wobble.

The knob maps linearly to the ceiling, +12 dB at 0 (speech never reaches it) to −8 dB at
1. An adaptive baseline — tracking the least bassy balance seen as "far" — was designed and
rejected: a singer who starts close and stays close would never be corrected, and the
behaviour would depend on history in a way an operator cannot reason about. The ceiling is
deterministic, and the balance meter exists so it can be set by looking.

### 4.4 The standalone opens no input on its first run

JUCE's stock standalone opens the default input *and* output before creating its window.
When they are different CoreAudio devices (the built-in mic and speakers are, on Apple
Silicon) it wraps them in an `AudioIODeviceCombiner`, and on a machine with several
virtual audio devices that combiner — and, on the machine this was built on, *any* input
open, even a single duplex virtual device — blocks indefinitely in coreaudiod with no
window, no CPU and no crash report. First found on Contourtonist; reproduced here on the
first launch, and `ffmpeg -f avfoundation` hung on every input device the same afternoon,
so it is the machine's input path, not JUCE. `Source/StandaloneApp.cpp` seeds a saved
audio setup with an output and no input the first time; the operator picks the mic in
Audio Settings and the choice is saved. JUCE's own "mute input" default stays on top of
that.

Consequence: the standalone has been launched and screenshotted, never run on a mic.
`tools/proxhost --show` is how the editor was watched on real audio instead — the built
plugin, the real editor, a file streamed in real time, no audio device at all.

### 4.5 CMake: architectures must be set before `project()`

`CMAKE_OSX_ARCHITECTURES` set after `project()` is accepted silently and produces an
arm64-only binary. **Verify with `lipo -archs`, never the log.** The release workflow
asserts it.

### 4.6 Testing in-place processing

`Compensator::process` works in place. A test that runs the same buffer through twice is
feeding the first run's output back in — a `silence` vector that came back holding the
shelf's tail looked like a hold that leaked. Refill buffers between runs.

## 5. Release plumbing

The workflow builds Linux x64/arm64, Windows x64/arm64 and one universal macOS bundle,
runs `ctest` on every runner (the suite has no dependencies, so it runs everywhere), zips
the bundles, builds a multi-part `.pkg` and a `.dmg` on macOS via the vendored
`release-lib.sh`, and NSIS installers for Windows from the zips. macOS artefacts leave CI
ad-hoc signed; the fleet's autosign agent on the author's Mac re-signs and notarises them
after the release is published. The About window's facts (`StoatworksAbout.h`) come from
the website's `projects.json` via sync-about; a changed guide URL is edited there, then
synced.

## 6. Verifying a change

`ctest` for the compensator (read the numbers, not just the pass), `pluginval
--strictness-level 10` on the VST3 and the AU, `auval -v aufx Prx1 Alsg`, and for anything
that changes the sound, `proxstat` over the speech files with `trace` and a third-octave
comparison of far/close/corrected. `proxhost` against `proxstat` proves the plugin is still
the compensator. Anything a click exercises in the editor (the knob's drag, the About
panel) has been reviewed and screenshotted, not click-tested; say so when you touch it.
